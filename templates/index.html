What the colors mean

Brighter or warmer regions generally indicate areas that contribute more strongly to the selected model output. Cooler regions generally indicate lower relative activation. Interpret the colors together with the complete spiral drawing.

Where the model is focusing

The highlighted areas show image features that influence the model’s output. These may overlap with parts of the drawn spiral, its curves, turns, edges, or other visible image features. A highlighted area does not establish that a specific drawing irregularity exists.

How to read the spiral

Look at the heatmap alongside the original drawing. Observe the overall spiral shape and visible drawing characteristics rather than interpreting a single highlighted spot in isolation. The overlay explains model behavior; it is not an independent clinical assessment.

What the visualization cannot tell you

Grad-CAM does not diagnose Parkinson’s disease, measure disease severity, or establish the cause of a drawing feature. The highlighted regions are model explanations, not proof of a medical finding.

Research interpretation only

Grad-CAM visualizations show which image regions influence a model’s output. They are not a standalone diagnostic tool and should not be used to confirm or rule out Parkinson’s disease.

PROJECT INFORMATION

About SpiralSense

Project overview, model approach, and responsible-use information.

01

What is SpiralSense?

SpiralSense is an advanced AI clinical screening interface that explores AI-assisted screening of Parkinson’s disease from spiral hand-drawing images. It provides a model prediction and a visual explanation to help researchers inspect model behavior.

02

Model approach

The project uses an EfficientNetV2-S image-classification model. The application reports the existing model output, calibrated probability, and uncertainty-related indicators from its current inference pipeline.

03

Explainability

Grad-CAM produces a heatmap that highlights image regions associated with the model’s prediction. It can help visualize model behavior, but it does not establish causation or clinical validity.

04

Responsible use and limitations

SpiralSense is an assistive AI tool, not a standalone medical device. Predictions may be wrong or uncertain and must not be used alone to diagnose, rule out, or make treatment decisions about Parkinson’s disease.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Project Technical Documentation & FAQ

Clear, verified answers about SpiralSense, its models, datasets, evaluation, and limitations.

SpiralSense is an academic research application designed to explore AI-assisted screening of Parkinson’s disease using computerized spiral hand-drawing analysis. It applies an EfficientNetV2-S deep convolutional neural network alongside Monte Carlo (MC) Dropout uncertainty estimation, temperature calibration, and Grad-CAM visual interpretability heatmaps.

Important: SpiralSense is strictly an exploratory research and educational prototype. It is not an FDA-cleared or CE-marked medical device and cannot diagnose or rule out Parkinson’s disease.

The SpiralSense screening pipeline processes each drawing through five verified, sequential stages:

  1. Image Upload & Validation: The user selects a spiral drawing (PNG, JPG, BMP, or WEBP up to 10 MB).
  2. Preprocessing: The image is decoded in grayscale, resized to 224×224 pixels with antialiasing, converted to float32 retaining the 0–255 pixel range, and expanded to a 3-channel RGB tensor (src/common.py).
  3. Deterministic & Stochastic Inference: The drawing passes through the trained EfficientNetV2-S model to obtain a raw sigmoid probability. Concurrently, 20 stochastic MC Dropout passes run through the classifier to quantify prediction variability (src/predict.py).
  4. Temperature Calibration & Decision: The raw probability is scaled using a validation-fitted temperature (T ≈ 0.4049, artifacts/temperature.json). If calibrated probability ≥ 0.58 (58.0%), the output is classified as PARKINSON'S; otherwise HEALTHY.
  5. Explainability & Cleanup: Grad-CAM generates a gradient-weighted activation heatmap from the top_conv layer using the Jet colormap, while the uploaded temporary image file is immediately deleted from disk.

Verified from the Flask upload configuration in app.py:

  • Supported file formats: PNG (.png), JPEG/JPG (.jpg, .jpeg), BMP (.bmp), and WEBP (.webp).
  • Maximum file size: 10 Megabytes (MAX_CONTENT_LENGTH = 10 * 1024 * 1024 bytes).

Files exceeding 10 MB or containing unapproved file extensions are rejected automatically before processing.

No. SpiralSense cannot diagnose, confirm, or rule out Parkinson’s disease.

A clinical diagnosis of Parkinson’s disease requires comprehensive evaluation by licensed neurologists, incorporating formal motor examinations (such as the MDS-UPDRS scale), patient history, non-motor symptom evaluation, response to dopaminergic therapy, and neuroimaging (such as DaTscan). SpiralSense is an algorithmic research prototype evaluating visual drawing patterns and must never be used as a substitute for qualified medical care.

SpiralSense utilizes EfficientNetV2-S (small variant), loaded from artifacts/parkinson_effnetv2s_classweighted.keras.

The network architecture consists of an ImageNet-pretrained convolutional backbone coupled to a custom classification head:

  • Global Average Pooling layer (GlobalAveragePooling2D)
  • Classifier Dropout layer (Dropout)
  • Feature Dense layer (Dense(128, activation="relu"))
  • Feature Dropout layer (Dropout)
  • Final Sigmoid layer (Dense(1, activation="sigmoid")) outputting the probability of Parkinson’s disease.

EfficientNetV2-S was selected based on the project's Review-I specification and comparative architecture analysis. Compared to older CNNs such as VGG16/19 or standard ResNet architectures, EfficientNetV2-S introduces fused-MBConv layers and progressive regularization, delivering superior parameter efficiency (approx. 21.5M parameters vs. 138M in VGG16) and stronger feature representation on compact biomedical datasets with minimal risk of catastrophic overfitting.

Transfer learning is a machine learning paradigm where knowledge gained from solving a general problem is transferred to a specialized task. Rather than training a deep network from scratch with random weights—which requires tens of thousands of medical drawings—SpiralSense reuses convolutional feature extractors pretrained on ImageNet (1.4 million images). The network's early layers already know how to identify edges, curves, and textures; training fine-tunes higher-level filters to detect tremor micro-irregularities in spiral traces.

The differences are mathematical and interpretative:

  • Raw probability: The unadjusted numerical output directly from the model's final sigmoid neuron, ranging from 0.0 to 1.0. Modern deep networks tend to be overconfident, pushing raw scores toward 0.0 or 1.0.
  • Calibrated probability: Scaled probability adjusted using Platt temperature scaling (T ≈ 0.4049, documented in artifacts/temperature.json). Temperature scaling rescales the logit values before the sigmoid function to minimize negative log-likelihood (NLL) on validation data.

Important note: Temperature calibration aligns confidence scores with empirical error rates, but it does not make the score a clinical probability of medical diagnosis.

The decision threshold is the operational cutoff value used to convert continuous calibrated probability into a discrete binary classification. SpiralSense uses a verified default threshold of 0.58 (58.0%) in src/predict.py:

  • Calibrated probability ≥ 0.58 → PARKINSON'S
  • Calibrated probability < 0.58 → HEALTHY

This operating point was configured during model validation to prioritize sensitivity (identifying true positive tremor signals) while maintaining balanced specificity, rather than assuming a naive 0.50 cutoff. It is an algorithmic decision parameter, not an individual clinical risk threshold.

Monte Carlo (MC) Dropout approximates Bayesian uncertainty in neural networks. Standard neural networks disable dropout during testing, yielding only a single deterministic prediction. With MC Dropout, dropout layers remain active during inference, and the model runs 20 stochastic forward passes (MC_DROPOUT_PASSES = 20 in src/predict.py).

This generates a probability distribution:

  • Mean (μ): The average predicted probability across all 20 passes.
  • Standard Deviation (σ) & Variance (σ2): Measures predictive instability across subnetworks.

Low standard deviation indicates consistent subnetwork agreement, whereas high variance warns that the model is sensitive to subtle perturbations. MC Dropout is an engineering proxy for model variability, not a complete measure of clinical diagnostic uncertainty.

Two distinct datasets are verified and present in the project repository:

  1. Original Spiral Drawing Dataset (data/raw/drawings/spiral/): The benchmark dataset widely studied in machine learning literature (Zham et al. / Kaggle Parkinson's Drawing Dataset), used for model development and internal evaluation.
  2. NewHandPD Dataset (data/raw/newhandpd/): An independent Brazilian clinical hand-drawing dataset (Pereira et al.), containing spiral drawings from Parkinson's patients and healthy controls, used strictly as an external evaluation cohort.

Note: Wave drawings also exist in raw repository storage, but SpiralSense specifically focuses on spiral drawing analysis.

Verified image counts from repository storage audits:

  • Original Spiral Dataset: 102 total images
    • Training split: 72 images (36 Healthy, 36 Parkinson's)
    • Testing split: 30 images (15 Healthy, 15 Parkinson's)
  • NewHandPD External Benchmark: 264 total images
    • Healthy control group: 140 images
    • Parkinson's patient group: 124 images
  • Same-test comparison cohort: 98 held-out images evaluated in artifacts/same_test_comparison.

Verified directly from src/common.py (load_image()):

  • Grayscale reading: Decoded as a single-channel grayscale image (channels=1).
  • Resizing: Resized to 224×224 pixels with antialiasing interpolation.
  • Float conversion: Cast to tf.float32 while preserving the standard 0–255 numerical pixel range (EfficientNetV2 incorporates internal rescaling).
  • 3-channel expansion: Converted from grayscale to RGB (tf.image.grayscale_to_rgb) to feed the 3-channel input expected by the backbone.

No synthetic data augmentation, morphological erosion, or bilateral filtering is applied at test-time inference.

In the original spiral drawing dataset, the benchmark was split into 72 training images (36 healthy, 36 Parkinson's) and 30 testing images (15 healthy, 15 Parkinson's). Additionally, project experimentation documented in PROJECT_NOTES.md and evaluate_corrected_split.py investigated patient-level 70/15/15 partitions to guarantee that all drawings from a single individual remain confined to a single split.

The project employs three verified safeguards to prevent overoptimistic evaluation:

  • Patient-level grouping: Evaluating split configurations where all drawings from a given participant identifier stay grouped within either training or testing, preventing identity memorization.
  • Split-isolated calibration: Temperature calibration (T ≈ 0.4049) was fitted strictly on validation split cross-entropy loss, ensuring the final test split remained completely untouched during hyperparameter tuning.
  • Independent external evaluation: Testing against NewHandPD (264 drawings collected at an independent clinic with different scanners and cohorts) eliminates any possibility of internal partition leakage.

Grad-CAM (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping) is an explainability method for convolutional neural networks. It computes the mathematical gradients of the model's output score with respect to feature maps in the final convolutional layer (top_conv in EfficientNetV2-S). Weighting feature maps by these gradients produces a 2D activation heatmap that highlights which spatial regions in the image most strongly guided the model's classification.

SpiralSense uses Matplotlib's continuous Jet colormap, normalized per image from 0.0 (minimum relative activation) to 1.0 (maximum relative activation):

  • Blue / Purple: Lower relative activation (minimal influence on prediction).
  • Cyan: Lower-to-moderate relative activation.
  • Green / Yellow: Moderate-to-higher relative activation.
  • Orange: High relative activation.
  • Red / Pink: Strongest relative activation (greatest influence on prediction).

Important note: The heatmap reflects relative attention for that specific drawing; it does not represent an absolute disease severity score or a clinical abnormality index.

No. A red or highlighted region simply indicates that the convolutional neural network focused heavily on the visual features in that area when generating its output score. It is not biological proof of a tremor, nor does it confirm the presence of Parkinson’s disease pathology.

The final convolutional layer of EfficientNetV2-S outputs feature maps with coarse spatial resolution (a 7×7 grid). To display an overlay on the original drawing, this map is upsampled via bilinear interpolation to 224×224 pixels and blended with the original drawing using an alpha transparency overlay (approx. 40% heatmap, 60% original image). This results in smooth, luminous gradients rather than razor-sharp pen contours.

In SpiralSense, model prediction and Grad-CAM generation are structured as independent execution blocks in app.py. If an error occurs during Grad-CAM generation (e.g. GPU memory exhaustion or tensor formatting), the prediction results—including classification, probabilities, and MC Dropout uncertainty—remain completely accessible, and an explanation notice is displayed.

SpiralSense enforces strict data minimization verified in app.py:

  • Uploaded drawings are saved temporarily to a secure unique path in the uploads/ directory solely for the duration of inference.
  • Immediately after prediction and Grad-CAM generation finish, the temporary image file is automatically deleted from disk in a finally block (temp_path.unlink(missing_ok=True)).
  • No uploaded drawings are permanently stored, cataloged, or transmitted to third-party cloud services.

Verified technical limitations include:

  • Static 2D images: Analyzes only static visual scans; it does not capture temporal dynamic signals such as drawing speed, pen-tip acceleration, pressure, or in-air hesitation times.
  • Small sample sizes: Developed on 102 spiral drawings and evaluated on 264 external samples, representing a limited demographic and clinical range.
  • Binary scope: Focuses only on Parkinson’s vs. Healthy; it cannot perform differential diagnosis against Essential Tremor or other neurological disorders.
  • No clinical trial validation: Has not undergone prospective clinical trials in healthcare settings.

Potential future research directions include:

  • Integrating digital digitizing tablet sensor data (measuring pen speed, acceleration, pen-up time, and pressure dynamics).
  • Expanding multi-center clinical validation across diverse patient age groups and disease stages.
  • Developing multi-class differential screening for Essential Tremor and atypical parkinsonian syndromes.
  • Incorporating Conformal Prediction sets with mathematical coverage guarantees for selective abstention on borderline cases.
